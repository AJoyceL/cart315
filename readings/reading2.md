What I liked the most about Schön’s *Reflective Conversation with Materials* is that he doesn’t treat a designer’s intention as something final, that it should lead directly to the finished result —that it is more of a proposal than a contract set in stone. Instead, he sees each mistake as an opportunity to lean. When something doesn’t go as expected, it doesn’t mean the designer is bad. It reveals something new about the problem and becomes part of the learning process. 

I resonated with this as a digital artist. When I’m drawing or practicing, I sometimes reach a point where something just feels wrong but I can’t immediately figure out why; just that it doesn’t feel right. For example, I’ve spent days trying to fix the proportions or the pose of my characters, only to realize that I don’t understand the anatomy as well as I'd assumed. That I knew, in theory, what I was doing, but I couldn’t apply that knowledge. In those moments, I get frustrated and feel I'm stuck, that whatever I'm doing won’t ever improve despite all the time I spent practicing. Schön’s ideas made me think about these moments differently. My failures aren’t proof that I'm a bad artists. It’s showing me exactly where I still have something to learn.

This also reminds me that I’m never really *finished* being an artist. That it isn’t the result, but the process. Every drawing gives me something to notice, practice, and improve. I might struggle with anatomy in one drawing and rendering in another, but each difficulty becomes a part of my learning curve. It becomes a reminder, being stuck isn’t a death sentence; it only means that I've reached a bridge that I haven’t learned how to cross yet.
